Possible side effects

When people report side effects, they are usually mild:

  • Upset stomach or nausea
  • Headache or dizziness
  • Feeling over-stimulated, especially if taken late in the day
  • Allergic reaction (rare): stop and seek help if you get a rash, swelling or trouble breathing

Starting with half a serving and taking it with a drink in the morning reduces most of these issues.

The biggest risk: poor-quality products

Raw shilajit can contain heavy metals such as lead or arsenic, plus fungi and other contaminants. That is why purification and third-party lab testing are essential. Read how to spot fake or low-quality shilajit.

Whatever brand you choose, ask for lab results. Our current pick is Pure Himalayan Shilajit; check what testing information the official site provides before you order.

Who should avoid shilajit

  • Pregnant or breastfeeding women
  • People with hemochromatosis or high iron levels
  • People with gout or high uric acid, unless a doctor approves
  • Children

Possible interactions

Shilajit may interact with some medications, including those for blood pressure and blood sugar. If you take any regular medication, ask your doctor or pharmacist before using it.

How to use it safely

  1. Buy only purified, lab-tested shilajit.
  2. Start with half a serving for a week.
  3. Follow the label and do not exceed the recommended dose.
  4. Stop if you notice side effects and speak to a doctor.

This article is general information and not medical advice. Always consult a qualified health professional about supplements.

Frequently asked questions

What are the most common side effects of shilajit?

Mild ones such as an upset stomach, headache or feeling too energized, usually when starting with a high dose. Starting low helps.

Can raw shilajit be dangerous?

Yes. Raw or unpurified shilajit can contain heavy metals, fungi and other contaminants. Only use purified, lab-tested products.
