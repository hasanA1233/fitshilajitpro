Shilajit Benefits: What the Research Actually Says

Updated November 19, 2026 · 3 min read

There is a lot of hype online. This guide separates what small studies have found from what is still just tradition.

In this article
  1. Energy and fatigue
  2. Testosterone in men
  3. Strength and training
  4. A source of trace minerals and fulvic acid
  5. Healthy aging and cognition
  6. The bottom line

Shilajit has a long traditional history, but modern research on it is still young. That is not a reason to dismiss it, but it is a reason to be careful with big claims. Here is a balanced summary.

1. Energy and fatigue

The most common reason people take shilajit is daily energy. Fulvic acid and the mineral content are often credited for this, and traditional use centers on vitality. Some small studies have looked at fatigue, and many users report feeling more energetic, but the evidence is still early.

2. Testosterone in men

One of the most cited studies (published in the journal Andrologia in 2016) gave purified shilajit to healthy men aged 45 to 55 for 90 days. The group taking shilajit showed higher total testosterone levels compared with placebo. It was a small study funded with industry involvement, so it is encouraging rather than conclusive. More detail is in our shilajit for men guide.

3. Strength and training

A small trial in recreationally active men found that those taking a higher dose of purified shilajit kept more of their muscle strength after a fatiguing exercise test than the placebo group. For people who train, that is interesting, but it is one study. See shilajit for athletes and workouts.

4. A source of trace minerals and fulvic acid

Shilajit naturally contains fulvic acid and a broad range of trace minerals. The amounts of each mineral are small, so it is not a replacement for a balanced diet, but it is one reason the resin is popular as a daily supplement. Learn more in shilajit and fulvic acid explained.

5. Healthy aging and cognition

Lab and animal studies have explored antioxidant effects and the brain, and you will often see these mentioned online. Human evidence here is very limited, so treat any strong claim about memory or anti-aging with caution.

The bottom line

  • Promising: small human studies on testosterone and strength retention.
  • Traditional, less studied: energy, vitality and overall wellbeing.
  • Unproven in humans: memory, anti-aging and disease claims.

If you decide to try it, choose a purified, lab-tested product, take it consistently for at least 8 weeks and pay attention to how you feel.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to notice shilajit benefits?

It varies. The studies that reported effects usually ran for 8 to 12 weeks of daily use, so consistency matters more than a single dose.

Is shilajit proven to work?

Not in the way a medicine is. A handful of small human studies show promising results, but larger, independent trials are still needed.
