Altai vs Himalayan Shilajit: Does the Source Matter?

Himalayan shilajit is the best known, but Altai shilajit has a strong following. Here is how they compare.

Shilajit is found in several mountain ranges. The two names you will see most are Himalayan and Altai.

Himalayan shilajit

Collected in the Himalayas across India, Nepal and Pakistan, it has the longest history in Ayurveda and appears in much of the published research. It is widely available, which also means quality varies enormously.

Altai shilajit

The Altai mountains sit where Russia, Kazakhstan, Mongolia and China meet. Part of the region, the Golden Mountains of Altai, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Altai shilajit (sometimes called mumijo) has a long tradition in Central Asian practice and is valued for its remote, clean environment.

If you would rather try a Himalayan resin, our current pick is Pure Himalayan Shilajit. As with any brand, check the product and testing details on the official site before you buy.

How they differ

The composition of shilajit depends on local plants, rock and climate, so mineral and fulvic acid levels vary between regions and even between harvests. Marketing often claims one region is superior, but there is no strong evidence that either is clearly better.

What really matters

  • Purification: how the raw material is cleaned and filtered.
  • Lab testing: heavy metals, purity and quality markers like DBPs.
  • Transparency: a clear source and an honest seller.

Frequently asked questions

Is Altai shilajit better than Himalayan?

Neither is automatically better. Purification, lab testing and honest sourcing matter more than the mountain range on the label.

Why is Himalayan shilajit so popular?

It has the longest history in Ayurvedic practice and most of the early research used Himalayan material, so it became the default name.
