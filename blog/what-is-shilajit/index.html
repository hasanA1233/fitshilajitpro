What Is Shilajit? The Complete Beginner's Guide

Updated November 19, 2026 · 3 min read

A dark, sticky resin from high mountain rock that has been used for centuries. Here is what it actually is, what it contains and how to start.

Shilajit is a thick, blackish-brown resin that seeps out of rock cracks in high mountain ranges such as the Altai, the Himalayas and the Caucasus, usually during warm summer months. It has been part of Ayurvedic and traditional Central Asian practice for a very long time, where it was valued as a daily tonic.

Where does shilajit come from?

Researchers believe shilajit forms over hundreds of years as plant material, mosses and microbes break down and become trapped between layers of rock. Pressure, temperature and time turn that material into a dense, mineral-rich substance. When the rock warms up, the resin slowly oozes out and can be collected.

The raw material is never eaten as-is. It contains rock, sand and other impurities, so it has to be dissolved, filtered and dried before it is safe to use. This purification step is the main difference between a quality product and a risky one.

What is inside shilajit?

  • Fulvic acid – the compound most people talk about. It is a natural organic acid found in soil and humus.
  • Humic substances – larger organic molecules related to fulvic acid.
  • Trace minerals – quality products are often described as containing 85+ minerals in small amounts, including iron, zinc, magnesium and others.
  • Dibenzo-alpha-pyrones (DBPs) – compounds that some brands test for and use as a quality marker.

The exact makeup changes from one mountain to another, which is why the source and lab testing matter so much.

The forms you will find

Shilajit is sold as resin (the most traditional form), tablets or capsules, powder and liquid drops. Resin is the least processed and easiest to judge for authenticity, while tablets are the most convenient. We compare them in detail in our resin vs capsules vs powder guide.

Why do people take it?

People typically add shilajit to their routine for daily energy and vitality, as part of a training routine, or simply as a mineral-rich supplement. A small number of clinical studies have looked at specific outcomes, but research is still limited, so it is best seen as a traditional supplement rather than a medicine. How to start

Most people take a pea-sized amount of resin dissolved in warm water, tea or coffee once a day. Start with a smaller amount for the first week and always follow the label. Our step-by-step guide to taking shilajit resin covers dosing, timing and storage.

Safety first: only buy purified, lab-tested shilajit. Raw or unverified products can contain heavy metals or other contaminants. Talk to your doctor before use if you are pregnant, breastfeeding, have a medical condition or take medication.

Frequently asked questions

Is shilajit a plant or a mineral?

Neither, exactly. It is an organic-mineral substance: plant matter that decomposed over centuries and mixed with minerals from the rock around it.

What does shilajit taste like?

Strong, earthy and slightly bitter, a bit like smoky tar or very dark tea. Most people dissolve it in a warm drink to soften the taste.
