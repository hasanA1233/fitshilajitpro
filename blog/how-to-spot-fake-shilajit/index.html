Fake shilajit can be anything from tar-like mixtures to resin bulked up with fillers. Poorly purified real shilajit can also be a problem, because raw material may contain contaminants. Here is how to protect yourself.

1. The temperature test

Real resin is hard and brittle when cold and becomes soft and sticky when warm. Put a little in the fridge: it should harden. Warm it in your fingers: it should soften and become pliable.

2. The water test

Drop a small piece into warm water. Real shilajit dissolves completely, turning the water golden-brown, without leaving grit or a layer of residue at the bottom.

3. The flame test

Real shilajit does not catch fire easily. When heated, it tends to bubble rather than burn with a big flame.

No home test replaces a lab report, so ask for one whichever brand you choose. Our current pick is Pure Himalayan Shilajit; check what testing information the official site provides before you order.

4. Smell and taste

Expect a strong, earthy, slightly smoky smell and a bitter taste. A sweet, chemical or completely neutral product is suspicious.

5. Lab testing and verification

This is the most important check. Look for brands that publish third-party lab results, test for heavy metals and use quality markers such as DBP verification. Home tests cannot detect contamination; lab tests can.

6. Source and transparency

Trustworthy sellers state where the shilajit comes from (for example, the Altai mountains), how it is purified and how to contact them. Vague origins and no contact details are red flags. Compare origins in Altai vs Himalayan shilajit.

Quick checklist

  • Hardens when cold, softens when warm
  • Dissolves fully in warm water
  • Earthy smell, bitter taste
  • Third-party lab tests and heavy-metal testing
  • Clear source and purification process

Frequently asked questions

Is cheap shilajit always fake?

Not always, but very cheap resin is a red flag. Proper purification and lab testing cost money, so prices far below the market average deserve suspicion.

Can home tests prove shilajit is safe?

No. Home tests can hint at authenticity, but only a lab test can confirm purity and check for heavy metals.
