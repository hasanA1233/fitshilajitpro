Why people choose shilajit

A traditional resin with a modern following

Here is why people add shilajit to their routine, and what the evidence does and does not say.

Daily energy

Traditionally taken to support stamina and vitality as part of a daily routine.

Training & recovery

Popular with active people. A few small studies are promising, but research is limited.

Trace minerals

Naturally contains a broad range of trace minerals picked up from mountain rock.

Fulvic acid

Its best-known compound, studied for how it binds and carries minerals. Human evidence is still limited.

Centuries of tradition

Used for generations in Ayurvedic and Central Asian practice as a daily tonic.

Simple to use

A pea-sized amount of resin dissolved in a warm drink, usually once a day.

What's inside

Fulvic acid & trace minerals

Shilajit forms over centuries as plant matter breaks down between mountain rocks. What ends up in the resin depends on where it comes from and how it is purified.

Fulvic acid

The main organic compound in shilajit. It is small and water-soluble, and researchers have studied how it binds minerals. Most of that work is in the lab, not in people.

Trace minerals

Shilajit naturally carries minerals such as iron, magnesium and zinc in small amounts. It is a traditional supplement, not a replacement for a balanced diet.

FAQ

Questions

What is shilajit?

A natural mineral-rich resin that seeps from high mountain rocks, formed over centuries from plant matter. It contains fulvic acid and many trace minerals.

How do I know it is real shilajit?

Real resin is hard when cold, softens in your hand and dissolves fully in warm water. Most importantly, choose products with third-party lab results.

How do I take shilajit resin?

Dissolve a pea-sized amount in warm water, tea or coffee once a day, usually in the morning. Always follow the label.

How long does a 20 g jar last?

About 65 servings, roughly two months at one serving a day.

Who should not take shilajit?

Pregnant or breastfeeding women, children, and people with high iron levels. Talk to your doctor first if you have a medical condition or take medication.
